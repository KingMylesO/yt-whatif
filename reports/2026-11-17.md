## Decisions and notes
- **Length:** wrote ≥1,300 words for tubes (1,311). Shrink and cosmic came in at 7:54 / 7:50 on the first voice pass, so I added sourced facts (silicon atoms across a hair, electron microscopes, the South Atlantic Anomaly/Hubble, multi-bit upsets, IBM's 1997 copper switch, Gelsinger's 2001 heat warning, Hess 1912, the Oh-My-God particle, clean rooms) → final 8:45 / 8:13 / 8:16.
- **Accuracy calls:**
  - **Hedged or attributed:** the Belgian glitch is "the leading explanation"; Mario 64 is "a popular theory, never proven"; Voyager 2's cause is "radiation is the usual suspect", since JPL never proved it.
  - **Cosmic rays vs faulty hardware:** the Google 2009 and Mozilla 2026 figures say that many errors come from faulty memory, not space.
  - **Lilienfeld:** "as far as anyone knows, he never got a working version".
  - **"3 nm":** described as a marketing name; 48 nm / 24 nm are IRDS roadmap figures.
  - **My own arithmetic:** the 160 GW, 150 pools, >6 failures/s and 300,000 atoms figures, flagged in the claim tables.
  - **Left out (weak sources):** Qantas Flight 72, which the ATSB did not attribute to cosmic rays; an "over 1,000 chips per car" figure (no reliable source); and the ASML "220,000 °C" figure, which sources disagree on, so the script says "hotter than the surface of the Sun".
- **No logos or likenesses:** real people and companies are named in text only. The phone and laptop are generic, the jet is a generic silhouette, and the game character is a generic cube robot, not Mario.
- **Visual variety:** tubes = warm walnut/amber with glowing vacuum tubes; chip = indigo/neon isometric micro-city with a tiny cyan "explorer" and scale bar; cosmic = crimson-black with phosphor-green memory-bit grids and particle streaks. Three new music presets (`tubes`, `chip`, `cosmic` + Short variants).
