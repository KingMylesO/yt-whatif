# The Crush Files #1 — What Your Brain Does When Your Crush Texts Back

One-off Short made on 2026-11-20 at the owner's request (the series normally runs on Fridays). Video name `short_cf01_text`; narration `pipeline/narration_cf01.py`, scenes `pipeline/scenes_cf01.py` (Kai and the Crush from `mc.py`, bedroom set from `kit3d.py`).

## Script (≈125 words, ★ = like-and-subscribe line)
1. Your crush just texted back. Here's what your brain just did.
2. Before you even open it, there's suspense. And your brain's reward chemical, dopamine, loves suspense. In one monkey study, dopamine cells stayed most active when a treat was fifty-fifty.
3. Then you see their name. When scientists scanned people who were intensely in love, a photo of their person lit up the brain's dopamine-rich reward areas.
4. ★ Like and subscribe for more Crush Files. Now adrenaline kicks in. Your heart speeds up, and blood is pulled away from your stomach. Hello, butterflies.
5. And rereading it ten times? One small study found people newly in love had serotonin changes a lot like people with OCD.
6. Your whole brain just reacted. More Crush Files on the channel.

| Claim | Source |

| In monkeys, dopamine neurons' sustained activity was highest when a reward had a 50% chance (maximum uncertainty) | Fiorillo, Tobler & Schultz (2003), *Science* 299:1898–1902, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12649484/) · [PDF](https://www.pdn.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/2003-fiorillo-science.pdf) · [Nature Reviews Neuroscience commentary](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn1127) |

(2005), *Journal of Neurophysiology* 94:327–337, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15928068/) · [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2005/06/050607031344.htm) |
| Excitement triggers adrenaline: heart rate rises and blood flow shifts away from the gut, felt as "butterflies" | [The Conversation: why do we get butterflies in our stomachs?](https://theconversation.com/explainer-why-do-we-get-butterflies-in-our-stomachs-72232) · [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/health/what-causes-a-nervous-stomach) |
| A small study (20 people in love within the last 6 months, 20 with OCD, 20 controls) found lower platelet serotonin-transporter density in the in-love group, similar to the OCD group | Marazziti, Akiskal, Rossi & Cassano (1999), *Psychological Medicine* 29:741–745, [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/alteration-of-the-platelet-serotonin-transporter-in-romantic-love/9599BA293019DFA6D05B315803B6D2A7) |

Accuracy notes: the monkey result is about sustained (not phasic) dopamine activity and is told as "one monkey study". The serotonin study is small, told as "one small study", and a published letter questioned its sampling; the narration doesn't claim love *is* OCD.
