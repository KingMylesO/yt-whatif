## In production (batch 07)

### 1. What If Earth Had Two Moons? (space, long-form `b7_moons`) — HAPPENING NOW
- **Hook:** On 29 October 2026 the Minor Planet Center announced that Earth has quietly captured a second, tiny moon: 2026 QQ8, a boulder about one to two metres wide, bound to Earth since about April. What if our second moon were a big one?
- **Why it works:** Fresh news plus a rich hypothetical: double moonlight, tides many times stronger, eclipses, and a twist (a second moon is probably not stable; one hypothesis says the Moon's far side was built by a companion moon that hit it).
- **Confirmed / unknown:** designation and capture confirmed (MPC, cited by Space.com and EarthSky); exact size and exit date unknown (stays bound through December; "early 2027" is a writer's estimate).
- **Sources:** [Space.com, 5 Nov 2026: Earth has captured a new mini moon](https://www.space.com/astronomy/earth/earth-has-captured-a-new-mini-moon-but-it-wont-stick-around-for-long) · [Canup, Levison & Stewart 1999: evolution of a terrestrial multiple-moon system](https://www.boulder.swri.edu/~robin/cls99.pdf) · [UC Santa Cruz, 2011: the "big splat" (Jutzi & Asphaug, Nature)](https://news.ucsc.edu/2011/08/big-splat/)

### 3. What If the Moon Had Its Own Moon? (standalone Short `short_b7_moonmoon`)
- **Hook:** Can a moon have a moon? On paper, yes, but only a few moons in the Solar System could keep one.
- **Why it works:** A viral idea ("moonmoons") with a real paper behind it, and a true twist: a satellite Apollo 16 left around our Moon crashed after 35 days.
- **Sources:** [Carnegie Science: can moons have moons?](https://carnegiescience.edu/can-moons-have-moons) · [Physics Today: can moons have moons?](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/can-moons-have-moons) · [NASA via phys.org: bizarre lunar orbits](https://phys.org/news/2006-11-bizarre-lunar-orbits.pdf)

### 4. What If Every Volcano Erupted at Once? (Earth, long-form `b7_volcano`) — HAPPENING NOW + owner's backlog
- **Hook:** Since 27 October 2026 an underwater eruption off Iwo Jima has added about 50 hectares of new land. What if all ~1,350 potentially active volcanoes went off on the same day?
- **Why it works:** A backlog favourite tied to live news, with real maps, a "volcanic winter" built from Pinatubo and Tambora, the CO₂ myth busted, and the Siberian Traps as the scale twist.
- **Confirmed / unknown:** dates and area from NASA Earth Observatory and the Japan Coast Guard; how long the land lasts is unknown (2023's new land washed away within weeks).
- **Sources:** [NASA Earth Observatory: eruption extends Iwo Jima's beach](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/eruption-extends-iwo-jimas-beach/) · [USGS: how many active volcanoes are there?](https://usgs.gov/faqs/how-many-active-volcanoes-are-there-earth) · [USGS: Pinatubo](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/self/)

### 9. The Crush Files #2: Why You Blush Around Your Crush (Crush Files Short `short_b7_blush`)
- **Hook:** Your crush looks at you, and your face goes red. Here's why.
- **Why it works:** Everyone has felt it; nerves and adrenaline widen the face's vessels; Darwin's "most human of all expressions"; one Dutch study (Dijk et al. 2009) found blushers were mostly rated more favourably after a mistake.
- **Sources:** [Maastricht University: Dijk et al. 2009](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/the-remedial-value-of-blushing-in-the-context-of-transgressions-a/) · [The Psychologist (BPS): the puzzle of blushing](https://www.bps.org.uk/psychologist/puzzle-blushing) · [Darwin 1872, chapter 13](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Darwin/Darwin_1872_13.html)

### 10. The Crush Files #3: Why Your Crush Gives You Butterflies (Crush Files Short `short_b7_butterflies`)
- **Hook:** They walk in, and your stomach flips. What's actually going on in there?
- **Why it works:** Fight-or-flight pulls blood away from the gut; your gut has its own nervous system (200–600 million neurons); most vagus-nerve fibres carry signals up to the brain.
- **Sources:** [The Conversation: why do we get butterflies?](https://theconversation.com/explainer-why-do-we-get-butterflies-in-our-stomachs-72232) · [Furness 2014 (PubMed)](https://www.pubmed.gov/24997029/) · [Breit et al. 2018, Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5859128/)
