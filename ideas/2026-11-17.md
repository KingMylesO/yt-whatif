# Ideas — 2026-11-17 (batch 04, one-off TECH batch)

Owner's request (2026-11-17): this batch is about transistors, microchips, semiconductors and how computer hardware works, in the channel's usual What-If style. All 25 ideas are tech; the space/Earth/body mix is paused for this batch only.

## 1. What If the Transistor Was Never Invented? (in production, batch 04 long-form)
- **Hook:** ENIAC had about 17,500 vacuum tubes, weighed 30 tons and drew 150 kW. Your phone has 19 billion transistors.
- **Why it works:** A tangible alt-history with a great twist: the transistor's core idea was patented in 1925, 22 years before Bell Labs built one.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: ENIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIAC) · [Radiomuseum: ENIAC](https://radiomuseum.org/r/military_eniac_electronic_numerical_integrator_and.html) · [Computer History Museum: 13 sextillion transistors](https://computerhistory.org/blog/13-sextillion-counting-the-long-winding-road-to-the-most-frequently-manufactured-human-artifact-in-history/) · [ETHW: Bell demonstrates the transistor](https://ethw.org/Bell_Demonstrates_Transistor)

## 2. What If You Shrank Down Inside a Computer Chip? (in production, batch 04 long-form)
- **Hook:** The "3 nanometer" chip in a new phone has no feature that is 3 nanometers wide.
- **Why it works:** A Fantastic-Voyage tour through package, copper wiring, transistor fins and a gate insulator only a few atoms thick. Every stop is a real scale comparison.
- **Sources:** [Apple: M3 Max has 92 billion transistors](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/10/apple-unveils-m3-m3-pro-and-m3-max-the-most-advanced-chips-for-a-personal-computer/) · [IEEE Spectrum: cobalt and chip wiring (tens of km of copper, ~15 layers)](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cobalt-could-untangle-chips-wiring-problems) · [Intel 2007: high-k metal gate](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1065/intels-transistor-technology-breakthrough-represents)

## 3. What If a Cosmic Ray Flipped a Bit in Your Computer? (in production, batch 04 long-form)
- **Hook:** In 2003, a Belgian voting machine gave one candidate exactly 4,096 extra votes, 2 to the 12th power. A single flipped bit.
- **Why it works:** Invisible particles from exploding stars changing real-world numbers is irresistible, and it has real stories: Voyager 2, Mario 64, bitsquatting.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Electronic voting in Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting_in_Belgium) · [The Planetary Society: Voyager 2, it was a flipped bit](https://www.planetary.org/articles/2492) · [Schroeder et al. 2009: DRAM errors in the wild](https://research.google/pubs/dram-errors-in-the-wild-a-large-scale-field-study/)

## 4. What If Your Phone Ran on Vacuum Tubes? (in production, teaser Short)
- **Hook:** Rebuild a phone's 19 billion transistors as glass tubes and you'd need roughly 160 gigawatts.
- **Why it works:** Absurd-scale arithmetic that lands in 40 seconds.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: ENIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIAC) · [Radiomuseum: ENIAC](https://radiomuseum.org/r/military_eniac_electronic_numerical_integrator_and.html) · [Tom's Hardware: A17 Pro, 19 billion transistors](https://tomshardware.com/news/apple-a17-pro-3nm-iphone-15-pro)

## 5. The First Computer Bug Was a Real Moth (in production, standalone Short)
- **Hook:** On September 9, 1947, engineers taped a moth into the Harvard Mark II logbook: "First actual case of bug being found."
- **Why it works:** Everyone knows the word "bug"; few know the moth, and fewer know Edison used "bug" in 1878.
- **Sources:** [Smithsonian NMAH: Log book with computer bug](https://www.si.edu/object/log-book-computer-bug:nmah_334663) · [Computerworld: Did Grace Hopper invent "bug"?](https://www.computerworld.com/article/2515435/moth-in-the-machine--debugging-the-origins-of--bug-.html)

## 6. What If Every Chip on Earth Stopped at Once? (in production, standalone Short)
- **Hook:** The 2021 chip shortage alone cost carmakers about $210 billion in sales. Now imagine every chip switching off.
- **Why it works:** A fast "what still works?" list: wind-up clocks, bicycles, old tube radios.
- **Sources:** [CNBC: chip shortage to cost auto industry $210 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/09/23/chip-shortage-expected-to-cost-auto-industry-210-billion-in-2021.html) · [GlobalSecurity: MiG-25 tube avionics](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/mig-25-service.htm)

## 7. The Machine That Makes Your Chips Fires 50,000 Tin Droplets a Second (in production, standalone Short)
- **Hook:** EUV lithography blasts molten tin into plasma hotter than the Sun's surface to make 13.5 nm light.
- **Why it works:** The most extreme machine most viewers have never heard of.
- **Sources:** [ASML: the hottest thing about ASML machines](https://www.asml.com/en/discover/hot-machines) · [ZEISS: EUV mirror scaled to Germany](https://www.zeiss.com/semiconductor-manufacturing-technology/inspiring-technology/euv-lithography.html) · [Tom's Hardware: $380M High-NA tool](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/intel-shares-biggest-unboxing-video-ever-as-asmls-dollar380-million-high-na-lithography-machine-is-installed-in-oregon-fab)

## 8. What If Moore's Law Never Slowed Down? (in production, standalone Short)
- **Hook:** Clock speeds stopped climbing around 4 GHz in the mid-2000s. Why?
- **Why it works:** Explains the "power wall" and atoms-as-a-limit in under a minute.
- **Sources:** [Britannica: Moore's law](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/705881) · [Horowitz et al.: clock scaling stopped early 2000s](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/cs/hardware/2014-horowitz-2.pdf)

## 9. The Election Glitch That Added 4,096 Votes (in production, standalone Short)
- **Hook:** 4,096 is exactly 2¹². One bit flipped in Schaerbeek, Belgium, in 2003.
- **Why it works:** A clean, verifiable number that proves the "it was a bit flip" story.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Electronic voting in Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting_in_Belgium) · [Computerworld: the culprit could be cosmic rays](https://www.computerworld.com/?p=1679274)

## 10. What If a Cosmic Ray Hit Your Computer? (in production, teaser Short)
- **Hook:** About one muon passes through every square centimeter of you each minute.
- **Sources:** [HyperPhysics: atmospheric muons](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Particles/muonatm.html) · [Wikipedia: Soft error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_error)

- **Sources:** as idea 2.

## 12. What If Computers Still Used Punch Cards?
- **Hook:** A 1 TB drive would be billions of cards. How tall a stack?
- **Why it works:** Data-storage scale made physical.
- **Sources:** [IBM: the IBM punched card](https://www.ibm.com/history/punched-card) · [Computer History Museum: punched cards](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/punched-cards/2)

## 13. What If Your Computer Had No Cooling?
- **Hook:** Clock speeds stalled around 4 GHz partly because air-cooled chips hit about 100 W.
- **Why it works:** Thermal throttling, heat sinks and "why your laptop fan screams".
- **Sources:** [Horowitz et al.](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/cs/hardware/2014-horowitz-2.pdf) · [Wikipedia: Dennard scaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennard_scaling)

## 14. What If Chips Were Made of Something Other Than Silicon?
- **Hook:** The first transistor was germanium. Gallium nitride now runs phone chargers.
- **Sources:** [ETHW: Bell demonstrates transistor (germanium)](https://ethw.org/Bell_Demonstrates_Transistor) · [Wikipedia: Gallium nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallium_nitride)

## 15. What If a Speck of Dust Landed on a Chip Being Made?
- **Hook:** Chip fabs are far cleaner than hospital operating rooms, and a single particle can kill a chip.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Cleanroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleanroom) · [Halbleiter.org: wafer fabrication](https://halbleiter.org/en/waferfabrication/rawsilicon/)

## 16. What If You Turned Sand Into a Computer Chip?
- **Hook:** Chip-grade silicon is 99.9999999% pure or better: under one foreign atom per billion.
- **Sources:** [SFA Oxford: high-purity silicon](https://www.sfa-oxford.com/rare-earths-and-minor-metals/minor-metals-and-minerals/silicon-market-and-silicon-price-drivers/high-purity-silicon-market-and-price-drivers/) · [Halbleiter.org](https://halbleiter.org/en/waferfabrication/rawsilicon/)

## 17. What If RAM Never Forgot? (DRAM refresh)
- **Hook:** Your computer's memory forgets everything every few dozen milliseconds unless it's refreshed.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Dynamic random-access memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_random-access_memory) · [Wikipedia: Memory refresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_refresh)

## 18. What If You Could See a CPU Think in Slow Motion?
